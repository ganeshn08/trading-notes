The sequence of swing highs and lows. Everything else in
[[technical-analysis|technical analysis]] is read against it.

## The definitions

| Structure | Pattern | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| **Uptrend** | Higher highs, higher lows | Buyers absorbing supply |
| **Downtrend** | Lower highs, lower lows | Sellers absorbing demand |
| **Range** | Neither | Two-sided; the edges are the trade |

A trend is intact until the opposing swing point breaks. That's it — there's no
indicator involved, and none is needed.

## Break of structure

An uptrend making a lower low is a **break of structure**. It doesn't mean
reverse; it means the assumption changed. Most failed trades come from continuing
to trade the old structure after it broke.

This is the mechanism underneath [[head-and-shoulders|head and shoulders]] — that
pattern is just a break of structure with a specific shape and a memorable name.

> [!note] Timeframes disagree, and that's fine
> A daily uptrend can contain a 15-minute downtrend without contradiction. Trouble
> starts when you enter on one timeframe and manage on another. Decide which
> timeframe defines your structure before you take the trade, not after it moves
> against you.

## Where price is likely to react

Structure tells you *direction*; [[support-and-resistance|support and resistance]]
tells you *where*. Prior swing points are usually the most reliable levels,
because they're where positions were actually opened — unlike a round number or a
Fibonacci retracement, which are where people *expect* something to happen.
