Price levels where the order book has historically been thick enough to stop a
move. The useful framing isn't "lines on a chart" — it's **where positions
exist**.

## Why levels work at all

Three groups act at a prior high:

- Those who **sold there** and were right, waiting to sell again
- Those who **bought there** and were wrong, waiting to exit at breakeven
- Those who **missed** the move and are waiting for a retest

All three produce supply at the same price. The level isn't magic; it's an
accumulation of unfinished business.

Which also explains the decay: **every touch consumes orders.** A level tested
four times has fewer defenders than one tested once. Retail teaches that repeated
tests confirm a level. The order-flow logic says the opposite.

> [!warning] Levels are zones, not prices
> Anyone quoting support to two decimal places is fitting the past. Use a band,
> and size for the band — see [[position-sizing|position sizing]].

## Working with them

- Levels drawn on higher timeframes dominate lower ones
- A broken resistance frequently becomes support — the same unfinished business,
  now on the other side
- The strongest levels coincide with a [[market-structure|structural]] swing point,
  not with a round number

## Where this shows up

- [[head-and-shoulders|Head and shoulders]] — the neckline is a support level, and
  the pattern is only meaningful when it breaks
- [[moving-averages|Moving averages]] behave as dynamic levels for the same reason:
  enough participants watch them that behaviour clusters there
- [[2026-08-24-nifty-breakout|The Nifty breakout note]] is this idea, traded
