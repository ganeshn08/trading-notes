Reading price and volume to estimate what's likely next. Not prediction — a way
of framing probabilities.

The honest case for it: markets are made of participants with positions, and
positions create predictable behaviour at predictable prices. Someone who bought
at a level defends it. Someone trapped above it wants out at breakeven. That's
what [[support-and-resistance|support and resistance]] actually is, underneath
the lines.

The honest case against: most of what's published is unfalsifiable. A pattern
that "works unless it fails" isn't a pattern. This is why [[backtesting]] matters
more than the pattern library.

## The three layers

1. **[[market-structure|Structure]]** — where price is in its larger swing
2. **Location** — [[support-and-resistance|support and resistance]] levels worth acting at
3. **Trigger** — the specific entry: a [[head-and-shoulders|pattern]], or an
   indicator condition from [[rsi|RSI]] or [[moving-averages|moving averages]]

Skipping layer 1 is the most common mistake. A perfect trigger against the
prevailing structure is a losing trade with good aesthetics.

> [!warning] Indicators are derivatives
> Every indicator is a transformation of price. [[rsi|RSI]] and
> [[moving-averages|moving averages]] contain no information price doesn't
> already have — they compress it into something easier to read, and they lag.
> Treat them as a lens, never as a source.
