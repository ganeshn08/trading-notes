## The setup

Nifty pressing the 24,850 area for the fourth session. Prior swing high from
early August, so a genuine [[support-and-resistance|level]] rather than a round
number.

- [[market-structure|Structure]]: higher highs and higher lows on the daily since June — intact
- [[moving-averages|20 EMA]] rising underneath, price holding above it on pullbacks
- [[rsi|RSI]] at 61 — strong, not stretched

## The trade

| Entry | 24,862 on the break |
| Stop | 24,690, below the last swing low |
| Risk | 172 points |
| Size | per [[position-sizing|fixed fractional]], 1% of equity |
| Target | none fixed — trail the 20 EMA |

## What I got wrong

The fourth test. I wrote in [[support-and-resistance|the levels note]] that every
touch consumes orders, and then treated a fourth test as *confirmation* rather
than as a depleted level. Two contradictory beliefs held at once, and I acted on
the comfortable one.

It broke, ran 40 points, and reversed through the entry inside the hour. Stopped
at 24,690 for a full 1%.

> [!note] The process was fine
> Correct level, correct stop placement, correct size, and the loss was exactly
> the planned amount. A losing trade taken properly is not a mistake — the
> mistake was the reasoning about the fourth test, which is a separate thing from
> the outcome and the only part worth changing.

Worth testing properly: does breakout success rate actually decay with test count?
That's a specifiable rule, so it belongs in [[backtesting]] rather than in my
impressions.
